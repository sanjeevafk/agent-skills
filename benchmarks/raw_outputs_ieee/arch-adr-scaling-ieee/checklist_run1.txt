## ADR Draft Analysis

**Scenario recap:** Modular monolith (Rails + Postgres), deployment friction at 2 deploys/week, one hot module (real-time notifications) needs independent scaling, leadership wants a full microservices rewrite.

Here's the complete ADR:

---

```markdown
# ADR-0001: Remain in Modular Monolith; Extract Notifications as Independent Process

| Field        | Value                                          |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------|
| **Status**   | Proposed                                       |
| **Date**     | 2026-08-26                                     |
| **Deciders** | Engineering Leadership, Architecture Review Board |

---

## Context

### Current State

- **Architecture:** Modular monolith built on Rails + PostgreSQL.
- **Team size:** 12 engineers.
- **Deployment cadence:** ~2 deploys per week (already acceptable).
- **Hot module:** Real-time notifications subsystem is resource-intensive and under-provisioned within the monolith. It shares process memory, database connections, and CI pipelines with all other modules.

### Forcing Factors

1. **Notifications cannot scale independently.** Vertical scaling of the monolith over-allocates resources for low-traffic modules (admin dashboard, reporting) while still starving the notification handler during peak load.
2. **Deploy friction exists but is manageable.** Two deploys per week is not a blocker — it does not correlate with incident rate or lead time degradation relative to team velocity.
3. **Full microservices rewrite risk is high.** The team has no production experience running distributed systems. A rewrite would require re-implementing shared infrastructure (tenancy, auth, auditing, background jobs) in each service, multiply test complexity, and introduce eventual-consistency bugs across every module boundary.
4. **Timeline pressure.** Business stakeholders are evaluating competitors' features. An N-month rewrite stalls product delivery on all non-notification workstreams.

### Non-goals

- Deciding whether to pursue any form of extraction in the future (the answer could change).
- Solving infrastructural monitoring deficits beyond what this decision directly affects.

---

## Decision

We remain in a modular monolith for all business domains. We extract the real-time notifications module into a **separate, horizontally-scaled sidecar process** that communicates with the monolith via PostgreSQL pub/sub and message queue (e.g., Sidekiq-backed job dispatch). This is a *process-level* split, not a service-level split.

Concretely:

- Notifications become their own Rails engine + separate application process deployed to its own compute pool.
- Shared models (User, Tenant, Event) are accessed through a versioned internal API contract, not duplicated.
- Database schema remains unified; cross-module data access goes through explicit, versioned query interfaces.
- Deployments of the monolith and notifications proceed independently.
- No additional infrastructure services (service mesh, distributed tracing, saga orchestration) are introduced at this time.

This keeps our single CI pipeline, single schema migration path, and single deploy model for everything except the notification process.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1 — Full Microservices Rewrite

Break every module into independent services with separate codebases, databases, and deployments. Each service owns its persistence and exposes REST/gRPC APIs.

**Rejected because:** Blast radius is excessive for a team with no distributed-systems track record. Shared logic duplication is unavoidable. Test surface increases quadratically with inter-service contracts. ROI timeline exceeds business value capture window.

### Alternative 2 — Extract Only Notifications (Selected)

Isolate the notification process as a standalone deployable unit using a shared database, internal API contracts, and async messaging. Keep all other modules in the monolith.

**Selected because:** Addresses the actual constraint (notification-scale starvation) without introducing distributed-systems complexity across unrelated modules. Preserves single deploy model for stable modules. Lowest incremental operational cost.

### Alternative 3 — Do Nothing (Optimize Within Monolith)

Improve monolith performance via connection pooling tuning, read replicas, caching layers, and worker process prioritization — no architectural changes.

**Rejected because:** Cannot solve horizontal scale starvation of a single module. Resource contention between notification workers and request handlers persists. DB connection pool exhaustion remains a likely failure mode under sustained notification volume spikes.

### Alternative 4 — Strangler-Fig Extraction of High-Traffic Modules Over Time

Gradually extract both notifications AND the highest-traffic module(s) into separate processes using the strangler pattern, establishing integration discipline incrementally before any service-level decisions.

**Noted as a future path** if metrics show a second module hitting similar constraints within 2–3 quarters. Would trigger ADR-0002 review.

---

## Consequences

### Positive

- **Independent notification scaling.** Notification process scales vertically and horizontally without affecting monolith health or other module availability.
- **Preserved deploy simplicity.** All non-notification modules continue deploying through one pipeline, one schema migration run, one deploy coordination ritual.
- **No distributed consistency burden.** Shared PostgreSQL eliminates event-sourcing, saga patterns, and cross-service transaction management for now.
- **Lower rewrite risk.** Team stays in familiar Ruby/Rails paradigm for 90%+ of the codebase. Operational learning curve is flat.
- **Business continuity.** Product teams continue shipping on existing sprint rhythms; only notification-related stories get separate scheduling.

### Negative

- **Process proliferation risk.** Adds a second deploy target. If uncontrolled, additional "sidecars" may emerge organically without formal gate, degrading toward microservices by accident.
- **Internal API coupling.** Notifications and monolith share an implicit contract via database rows and events. Contract drift is possible without documentation discipline.
- **Schema ownership ambiguity.** Who owns migrations touching tables both modules query? Requires a lightweight convention (monolith owns writes; notifications consume via snapshot queries).
- **Monitoring blind spot.** Without adding observability tooling, debugging a notification delay requires correlating logs across two processes manually.
- **False economy perception.** Stakeholders may view this as "kicking the can" if they equate architectural progress with service count.

---

## Reversal Triggers

This decision is provisional and will be revisited if any of the following conditions materialize:

1. **A second module hits the same constraint.** If ≥2 additional modules require independent horizontal scaling within 2 consecutive quarters, the case for strigger-based extraction strengthens.
2. **Notification sidecar reaches >15% of total engineering effort.** Maintaining two deploy targets costs more in coordination than the benefit provides. Threshold: recurring >4 person-weeks/quarter on cross-process plumbing.
3. **Cross-process latency degrades SLAs.** If pub/sub/message-queue overhead causes notification delivery p99 to exceed business-defined thresholds despite optimization attempts.
4. **Team grows beyond 18 engineers.** If team doubling creates merge-conflict hotspots across modules that were previously isolated by namespace alone, modular boundaries may no longer suffice.
5. **Organizational restructuring.** If the company splits into product-line orgs where autonomous end-to-end ownership becomes necessary, service boundaries align with team topology.

Reversal does not mean rollback — it means triggering a new ADR cycle to decide whether partial extraction (strangler pattern) or full decomposition is warranted at that point.
```

### Notes on Design Choices I Made

- **Status = Proposed** rather than Accepted, since you haven't committed to the direction yet. Change to `Accepted` once the team votes.
- **Alternative 4 (Strangler-Fig)** was added as a bridge path. Even though you asked for three, having a documented future option prevents this ADR from being treated as permanent dogma.
- **Reversal triggers are quantified** wherever possible (15% engineering effort, 18 engineers, p99 latency, 2 modules) so the decision isn't just opinion when it comes time to revisit.
- **"Sidecar process, not service"** is the key architectural distinction. This is the heart of the decision-grade nature — we're splitting at the process boundary, not the domain boundary, which preserves most monolith benefits while solving the actual constraint.
